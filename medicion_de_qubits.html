Born's Rule for this Setup

P = |⟨b|ψ⟩|²
$$P(b) = |\langle b|\psi\rangle|^2 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad |\psi\rangle \xrightarrow{\text{measure}} |b\rangle$$

Rule 1

        
Outcomes are probabilistic

        

          The chance of outcome $b$ is the squared overlap $|\langle b|\psi\rangle|^2$. Only states lying exactly on the measurement axis give a certain answer.
        

      

      

        
Rule 2

        
The state collapses

        

          After measuring, the qubit becomes the state it reported. Measure again in the same basis and you get the same answer every time (Experiment 4).
        

      

      

        
Rule 3

        
The basis is a choice

        

          Hardware measures in the Z basis. To measure in X we apply $H$ first; for Y we apply $S^\dagger$ and then $H$. Z and X are incompatible: knowing one erases the other (Experiment 5).
        

      

      

        
Rule 4

        
One shot is not enough

        

          A single shot returns one bit and destroys the superposition. To learn $\alpha$ and $\beta$ we must prepare and measure the state many times — that is why Qiskit runs 1024 shots by default.
        

      

    


  


  
  

    

      ⏚
      Pedagogical Takeaway: Measurement is not a passive look: it picks an answer at random, following Born's rule, and leaves the qubit in that answer.
