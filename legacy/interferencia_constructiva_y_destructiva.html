Constructive

$$\Delta\varphi = 0^\circ, 360^\circ, \dots \;\Rightarrow\; R = A + B$$

The waves are in phase: crest meets crest and trough meets trough. The amplitude grows and the intensity can reach four times that of a single wave.

Destructive

$$\Delta\varphi = 180^\circ \;\Rightarrow\; R = |A - B|$$

The waves are out of phase: crest meets trough. With equal amplitudes they cancel completely and the result is a flat line.

Quantum Connection

$$P = |\alpha_1 + \alpha_2|^2 \neq |\alpha_1|^2 + |\alpha_2|^2$$

In quantum mechanics, amplitudes (not probabilities) are added, so they can interfere. Algorithms like Grover's use constructive interference on the right answer and destructive interference on the wrong ones.

∿ Pedagogical Takeaway: Phase decides everything: the same two waves can double each other or disappear completely.
